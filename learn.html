Digit contracts are simple to place and easy to misread. This covers what the instruments are, how a payout is priced, and where the edge really sits.

What a synthetic index is

A price series generated by a published random process, not a real-world asset. Nothing external moves it, no news, no earnings, no market open. Volatility 10 means roughly 10% annualised volatility; Volatility 100 moves ten times as hard. The number in the name is the whole story.

What the last digit is

Every tick prints a price to two decimals. The final decimal is the digit your contract settles on. Even/Odd reads its parity, Over/Under compares it to a barrier, Matches/Differs asks whether it lands exactly on one number.

How a payout is priced

Payout is set so the house keeps a margin at the true probability. Even/Odd pays 1.953× on a 50% chance: a fair price would be 2.0×, so about 2.3% of every stake is the cost of trading. That gap is constant and it compounds across every contract you place.

The five contracts, and the odds behind them

Each digit 0-9 is equally likely over a long run, so the true probability of every contract is fixed and knowable. This is what you are actually buying:

50%Even / Odd, five of ten digits. Pays 1.953×, so expected return is −2.35% per contract.
10%Matches, one exact digit. Pays 10.35×, expected return −2.35% at a true 1 in 10.
90%Differs, any digit but one. Pays 1.084×, wins nine times in ten and still runs a negative expectation.
40%Over 5, digits 6 to 9. A barrier changes the odds, and the payout moves with it.

A high win rate is not an edge

Differs wins nine times out of ten and still loses money over a long enough run. The only number that decides whether a strategy survives is expected return per contract, and on every one of these it starts negative.

Reading the digit history

What the percentages tell you

The circles under the chart count how often each digit appeared in the recent window. Over a short sample these will always look uneven, that is what randomness looks like. A digit showing 14% over 120 ticks is ordinary noise, not a signal.

The gambler's fallacy

Six odd digits in a row does not make even more likely on the seventh. Each tick is drawn independently, so the odds reset every time. The streak you are looking at has no memory and no obligation to correct itself.

Where a real edge would come from

Only from a genuine, persistent deviation in the generator, measured over thousands of ticks, not dozens, and large enough to clear the payout margin. The AI scanner ranks by expected return for exactly this reason, and most of the time it will honestly report no edge worth taking.

Managing the downside

Position sizing

Stake a fixed, small share of your balance, one to two percent is a common ceiling. It will not turn a negative expectation positive, but it decides how long you last and how much a bad run costs you.

Why the loss multiple is dangerous

Doubling after every loss feels like it guarantees a recovery. It does not. Six losses at ×2 means your seventh stake is 64 times the first, and a run of six is common. Martingale trades a high chance of a small win for a small chance of losing everything.

Set the targets before you start

Target profit, target loss and loss multiple sit on the trade panel for a reason. Decide all three while you are calm, and let the run stop itself. A limit you set mid-session is a limit you will move again.

Practise before you risk anything

Switch to the demo account from the balance button at the top. Same feed, same pricing, same contracts, virtual funds. Place a hundred contracts there and look at the total, it is the cheapest lesson on this platform.
